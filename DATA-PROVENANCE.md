# Data provenance and published terms

Inputs are retrieved directly from original version-pinned CRAN package archives by setup_sources.py; no raw observations or extracted annual inputs are mirrored in this repository.

Renext 3.1-4 declares GPL (>= 2) for the package software. In correspondence dated 18 September 2026, the Renext maintainer clarified that this declaration does not formally cover its bundled sea-surge data or missing-observation intervals. ASNR (formerly IRSN) compiled and processed these data from SHOM source observations and permits their reuse for scientific purposes, with citation of both Renext and ASNR. The maintainer specifies illustrative use only because the underlying observations and processing methodology have since been updated. Accordingly, this replication uses the archived Brest inputs only to reproduce the historical published example; the resulting return levels are not presented as current operational flood-risk estimates. This correspondence is not a blanket licence for unrestricted raw-data redistribution, and no raw observations or extracted annual inputs are mirrored here.

evmissing 1.0.2 declares GPL (>= 3). Plymouth Centre daily maximum ozone observations (1998–2024) originate from UK-AIR. Attribution: © Crown 2026 copyright Defra via uk-air.defra.gov.uk, licenced under the Open Government Licence (OGL). See https://uk-air.defra.gov.uk/about-these-pages and https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/ .

Archive URLs and exact SHA256 values are recorded in setup_sources.py and evidence/setup-receipt.json. Both original package DESCRIPTION notices are retained under notices/. Annual aggregation is performed locally. Published fitted tables and diagnostic graphics report this replication's analysis; source observations and original published comparison targets are not relicensed as our property.

The historical acceptance receipt's licence_cleared=false field is preserved as recorded at that test. Successful retrieval alone is not a rights determination; the Brest reuse basis is the scientific-use permission and restrictions described above, rather than an inference that the package GPL covers its data.
